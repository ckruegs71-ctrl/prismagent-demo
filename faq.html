Getting started

What does PrismAgent Solutions do?

We build custom AI systems that take the hand-typed work out of the jobs that make a business money: reports and proposals put together automatically, numbers pulled out of the software you already use, and calls and leads answered day or night.

Every system is built inside your own tools and templates, a person on your team checks what matters, and every build is measured in dollars before and after.

Where should I start if I have never done any automation?

Lead capture and response, in almost every case. It carries the biggest dollar losses, it's the fastest to build, and the results show up within days: calls that used to hit voicemail start turning into booked appointments.

Before building anything, find out how many calls you miss after hours and how many quotes went quiet last month. Those two numbers tell you where your money's leaking and give you a baseline to measure against. Skip the fancy stuff. Start where the bleeding is.

Can AI automation work for my type of business?

If your business takes inquiries, sends quotes or proposals, books appointments, serves clients, and benefits from reviews and referrals, then yes. Contractors, law firms, medical and dental clinics, agencies, accountants, and the trades all run on the same basic functions. Only the vocabulary and the deal sizes change.

A $250 service call and a $50,000 remodel need different follow-up timing, but both need follow-up that goes out without anyone remembering.

Do I need technical skills to use AI automation in my business?

No. The system gets built once, by someone who knows how, and then it runs in the background while you and your team work the way you already work. Your job afterward is reading a weekly report, responding when the system flags something for a human, and approving the occasional tweak. If you can read a text and click a link, you're qualified.

Do I need to change the software I use?

No. We build inside the tools and templates you already have, so your team keeps working the way it does today.

How do I know if AI automation is actually worth it?

Measure it in revenue, not in busy-looking numbers. Before we build anything, every project gets one number to move, stated as a baseline, a target and a date. Example: you get 5 leads a week today. If this build gets you to 15 within two months, was it worth it?

A properly built system reports those numbers to you automatically, so the answer to "is this working" shows up in your inbox every Monday instead of requiring an investigation.

How we work

Will the AI make mistakes with my numbers?

The math runs in code, never in the AI, and a person on your team checks what matters before anything goes out.

How long does it take to set up?

A single build, like missed-call text-back plus after-hours call answering, is typically live within two to four weeks. A fuller build usually lands somewhere in one to three months, depending on how many tools need connecting and how custom your sales process is.

Honestly, the slowest part of most projects isn't the technology. It's collecting your scripts, pricing and process details so the system sounds like your business instead of a template.

What if my team resists the change?

People resist automation when they think it's coming for their jobs, so lead with what it actually does: it kills the work they already hate. Nobody on your staff loves hand-texting reminders, chasing unfinished paperwork, or copying lead info between systems.

In my experience the resistance lasts right up until the first week the system catches a lead or fills a cancellation that would've been lost, because at that point the team sees it making their numbers better.

Should I hire a consultant or buy the software myself?

Buying software gets you raw materials. Hiring a consultant gets you a working system, built faster, without you burning your evenings learning automation software. The do-it-yourself route is real if you've got the time and the interest.

The honest tradeoff is time and failure risk: owners who build it themselves usually spend months getting to what an experienced builder delivers in weeks, and the most common ending is a half-finished account that quietly gets abandoned.

What is GoHighLevel and do I need it?

GoHighLevel is an all-in-one platform combining a CRM (the software that tracks your leads and clients), texting, email, calendars, automation and review management in one place. Whether you need it depends on what you're running now.

If you already have a CRM your team actually uses, tools like n8n or Zapier can usually add the automation on top. If your current setup is a spreadsheet and an inbox, GoHighLevel is probably your fastest path. The platform matters far less than what gets built inside it.
